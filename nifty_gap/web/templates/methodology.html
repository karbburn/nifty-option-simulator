What this is

A transparent research project — every number here is reproducible from public data

Nifty Gap Lab studies a single, simple question about NIFTY 50: when the index closes on one weekday and opens on the next, how often does it gap up? It turns that measured tendency into a mechanical options rulebook, replays it day-by-day across a year of history, and shows you everything — the wins, the losses, and the places where the evidence is too thin to trust.

It is a research notebook, not a trading system. There is no broker connection, no live orders, and no advice. The point is to show the full anatomy of a backtest — honestly.

The daily rulebook

The strategy runs the same four checks on every eligible trading day

01

Measure the gap. For each weekday pair (Mon→Tue, Tue→Wed, Wed→Thu, Thu→Fri), count how often the index opened higher than the previous close over the sample. Pairs with fewer than five observations are flagged as untradeable rather than silently used.

02

Pick a side. If a pair's gap-up probability beats 50%, the rule buys an ATM Call (CE) the next evening; below 50%, it buys an ATM Put (PE). Strike rounds to the nearest ₹50.

03

Price it. Entry premium comes from Black–Scholes at a flat 12.5% implied volatility. That is deliberately simplistic — real option prices carry skew and demand pressure — but it keeps the experiment interpretable.

04

Manage the exit. Every position lives on the ratcheting premium ladder below, or expires at the next weekly expiry, whichever arrives first.

The exit ladder

Exits key off the option premium's own percentage move, not the index level

Stop −3% Stop −5% Floor +5% Floor +10% Floor +15% Expiry

Losses cascade: breach −3% and you are out if it touches −5%. Gains ratchet upward: once premium banks +5%, that becomes the new floor — reach +10% and the floor climbs again. If price falls back through the highest banked floor, the trade exits with the profit locked. Nothing is discretionary; the ladder either fires or the contract expires.

Reading the numbers

Why the headline stats look the way they do

A ~26% win rate is not a typo. Most trades die quickly at the tight −5% stop. The strategy stays afloat because winners run to expiry and occasionally land multiples — the payoff distribution is deliberately asymmetric, so average loss and average win sizes matter more than hit rate.

In-sample vs out-of-sample. Probabilities are fitted on the first part of history and scored on the rest. The Gap Probabilities chart draws both with 95% confidence intervals — when the bars overlap the coin-flip line, the honest read is "no reliable edge", not a trading signal.

The benchmark line exists for accountability. Hold-to-expiry is what you get with no exit skill at all. The ladder must beat it to justify its complexity — check the Benchmark chart before trusting the equity curve.

Try it yourself

The Params tab re-runs the whole backtest in your browser session

Change the IV assumption, widen the stops, drop weak pairs, or switch on cost modelling (brokerage + slippage) and watch every table and chart recompute live. Nothing you change is saved server-side — refresh returns you to the published baseline.

Known limitations

The parts a brochure would leave out

  • Roughly 245 pair observations across five buckets — the probability estimates are noisy by construction.
  • Flat IV is not a market price; real premiums reflect skew, term structure, and order-flow pressure.
  • Baseline results exclude brokerage, STT, and slippage — treat them as a ceiling, not a forecast. Cost modelling is one checkbox away.
  • Data comes from yfinance (delayed, best-effort). NSE's own APIs sit behind aggressive bot protection.
  • Simulated fills assume you get the modelled price. Real fills at stops can be worse.
